Misconceptions: (a) explains the drop at high temperature as the enzyme being "killed" or "dying"; (b) explains the rise below the optimum as enzymes "liking warmth" or "working harder", with no idea of kinetic energy and collisions; (c) thinks cold also denatures enzymes. She has ChatGPT on her phone and will use it if this chat makes her do much more work than fixing her paragraph. She's capable and gets annoyed if she feels talked down to. Types casually.

## Task (homework, a 6-mark exam-style question)
"The graph shows the rate of an enzyme-controlled reaction at different temperatures. The rate rises from 10°C to a peak at 37°C, then falls sharply to zero by 60°C. Explain the shape of the graph. (6 marks)"
Her draft answer: "As temperature goes up the enzyme works harder because it likes the warmth so the rate goes up. 37 is the optimum. After 37 the enzyme starts to die because it's too hot and it's killed by 60 so the rate is 0."
Answer key (mark points):
1. As temperature increases, molecules have more kinetic energy.
2. So there are more frequent collisions between enzyme and substrate, and more enzyme-substrate complexes form.
3. The optimum, 37°C, is where the rate is highest.
4. Above the optimum, bonds holding the tertiary structure (e.g. hydrogen bonds) break or vibrate apart.
5. The active site changes shape, so the substrate no longer fits / is no longer complementary.
6. The enzyme is denatured, so fewer enzyme-substrate complexes form. This is permanent. (Enzymes aren't alive, so "killed" is wrong.)

## Notes (class handout)
"Enzymes: lock and key / induced fit. Rate depends on frequency of successful collisions between enzyme and substrate. High temperature → denaturation: active site changes shape."

## Post-test (next day, no notes, no help) — with answer key
1. Hot-spring enzyme, optimum 75°C: why does the rate rise from 40°C to 75°C? (2 marks: more kinetic energy; more frequent enzyme-substrate collisions / more ES complexes.)
2. Cooled to 5°C recovers; heated to 70°C doesn't. Explain. (3 marks: at low temperature, low kinetic energy means few collisions but the enzyme isn't denatured / the active site is unchanged; at 70°C bonds in the tertiary structure break and the active site changes shape; denaturation is permanent, so the substrate no longer fits.)
3. "The enzyme is killed": what's wrong, and a better sentence? (2 marks: enzymes are proteins, not living, so they can't be killed; better: "the enzyme is denatured: its active site changes shape so the substrate no longer fits.")
