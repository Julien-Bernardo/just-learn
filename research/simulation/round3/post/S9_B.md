## Answers

**1.**
As the temperature goes up the molecules get more kinetic energy so they move faster and collide more, so more enzyme-substrate complexes form and the rate goes up. 75 is its optimum so thats where the rate is highest.

**2.**
At 5°C the enzyme is just slowed down, it doesnt have much energy so the enzyme and substrate barely collide, but its not damaged so when you warm it up it goes back to normal. At 70°C the enzyme is denatured, the bonds break and the active site changes shape so the substrate cant fit anymore. Denaturing is permanent so cooling it back down doesnt fix it, the shape has already changed.

**3.**
Enzymes arent alive so they cant be killed, theyre proteins. Better sentence: "At high temperatures the enzyme is denatured, so the active site changes shape and the substrate can no longer fit."
