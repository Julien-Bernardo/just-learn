## Answers

**Question: How far was the Great Depression responsible for Hitler's rise to power?**

The Great Depression was largely responsible for Hitler's rise to power, but it was not the only reason. The Depression hit Germany from 1929 and unemployment reached about 6 million by 1932, which was significant because lots of desperate people stopped trusting the Weimar government and looked for someone extreme who promised solutions. This is shown by the Nazi vote, which went from only 2.6% in 1928 to 37% in July 1932, so it clearly made a massive difference. Propaganda from Goebbels also helped because it told people Hitler would give them jobs and scrap the treaty. However, the Depression was less important than the political deals at the top, because Hitler never won a majority and he only became Chancellor in January 1933 when Hindenburg and von Papen chose to appoint him. The treaty of Versailles had also left Germans angry and the economy weak since the hyperinflation in 1923, so people were already open to him. So the Depression was the main trigger for Hitler's rise, but it needed the other factors to actually put him in power.
