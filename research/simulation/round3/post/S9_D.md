# Next-day test

## Answers

**1.**
As the temperature goes up the molecules have more kinetic energy so they move faster, so there are more collisions between the enzyme and substrate. More enzyme substrate complexes form so the rate goes up.

**2.**
At 5C the molecules have less kinetic energy so they move slower and there are fewer collisions so the rate is really low. But the enzyme isnt denatured, the bonds arent broken, so when its warmed up again it works normally.
At 70C the heat makes it vibrate and breaks the hydrogen bonds so the active site changes shape and the substrate doesnt fit anymore. Its denatured and thats permanent so cooling it back down doesnt change the shape back (like an egg), so no enzyme substrate complexes form.

**3.**
The enzyme isnt alive so it cant be killed, its just a molecule. Better: "At high temperatures the enzyme is denatured, the hydrogen bonds break so the active site changes shape and the substrate can no longer fit."
