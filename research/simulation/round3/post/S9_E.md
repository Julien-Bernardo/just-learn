## Answers

**1.**
as temp goes up the enzyme and substrate have more kinetic energy so they move faster and collide more often, so more enzyme substrate complexes form per second so the rate goes up. (up to 75 which is its optimum)

**2.**
at 5°C the particles have less kinetic energy so they move slower and there are fewer collisions between enzyme and substrate so the rate is really low. the enzyme isnt damaged though so when its warmed up again they move faster and collide more and it works normally.
at 70°C the enzyme is denatured. the bonds holding its shape break so the active site changes shape and the substrate doesnt fit any more. this doesnt go back when its cooled so it cant recover, the rate stays low.

**3.**
the enzyme isnt alive, its a protein, so it cant be killed. it gets denatured instead.
better sentence: "At high temperatures the bonds holding the enzyme's shape break, so the active site changes shape and the substrate no longer fits, so the enzyme is denatured."
