**2.** What I know: u = 20 m/s, v = 0 (it comes to rest), t = 10 s. I'm not given a, and I want s. The equation that uses u, v and t is s = ½(u + v)t, so I'll use that. It's the same as taking the average speed.

s = ½ × (20 + 0) × 10
s = ½ × 20 × 10
s = 10 × 10
s = 100 m

I'm not 100% sure, so I'll check it another way. a = (v − u) / t = (0 − 20) / 10 = −2 m/s². Then s = ut + ½at² = 20 × 10 + ½ × (−2) × 10² = 200 − 100 = 100 m. It matches, so I trust it. (It's not 20 × 10 = 200, because the train is only going 20 m/s at the very start and then it slows down.)

**Answer: 100 m**

**3.** The final speed is only the speed at the very last instant. An accelerating object is going slower than that for the rest of the time, so if you do final speed × time you're pretending it was going that fast the whole time. That makes the distance too big. distance = speed × time only works when the speed is constant, which my sheet says too ("for constant speed"). If the speed is changing you'd need to use the average speed, or one of the other equations like s = ut + ½at².

**4.** What I know: u = 5 m/s, v = 15 m/s, s = 50 m. I want a. I don't know t and I don't need it, so I'll use the one with no t: v² = u² + 2as.

15² = 5² + 2 × a × 50
225 = 25 + 100a
225 − 25 = 100a
200 = 100a
a = 2 m/s²

Quick check: average speed = (5 + 15)/2 = 10 m/s, so t = 50 / 10 = 5 s. Then a = (15 − 5) / 5 = 2 m/s². Same answer.
