## Answers

**Question: How far was the Great Depression responsible for Hitler's rise to power?**

(working, scribbled at the side: point - depression big cause. evidence - 6 million, 2.6 to 37%. explain - people desperate. but - versailles / propaganda. link)

The Great Depression was an important reason for Hitler's rise to power, but not the only one. The Depression hit Germany from 1929 and by 1932 unemployment was about 6 million. Before this the Nazis were a small party and only got 2.6% of the vote in 1928, but in July 1932 they got 37%. This shows the Depression was important because people were desperate and lost faith in the government, so they voted for the Nazis who promised jobs and change. However, it wasn't just the Depression. Goebbels' propaganda helped Hitler get support, and the anger about the Treaty of Versailles was already there as well. Also Hindenburg and von Papen made Hitler Chancellor in January 1933, which was what actually put him in power. So the Depression was a big cause because it made the Nazis popular, but it was only partly responsible as other things like propaganda and the political deals also mattered.
