## Tutor behaviour critique
**Session X**
- Helped:
  - Asking for the gut answer first surfaced misconception (a) immediately.
  - The £100 ±50% test was quick, concrete and self-generated evidence.
  - At [3], switching to a short worked example instead of refusing again was the right rescue for a student about to leave.
  - Asking for the conclusion "in one line" kept the output small.
- Hurt:
  - The "20% lower exam scores" line is a lecture to a student whose profile says lectures make him switch tabs. Drop it.
  - "What's different about the 50% you took off and the 50% you added back?" was too open for Kai right after he typed "75". When he answered with just "75", the tutor should have asked something binary: "50% of 100 is 50. What's 50% of 50?"
  - The worked example gave away the reasoning sentence, so Kai's "why" became paraphrase. Give the bike numbers but leave the reason as a fill-in: "The 20% added back is 20% of ___".
- Missing:
  - Nothing addressed reverse percentages, which Kai would meet in Q4-8 and on the test. Rather than "send me Q4" (which he will not do), the tutor should have offered the fastest useful thing: "Got any 'original price' ones? Paste one. They're the trap. It's ÷1.1, not −10%." Kai would likely accept that as a time-saver.

**Session Z**
- Helped:
  - Setting the expectation ("about 2 minutes") and a deal up front.
  - Using Kai's own notes ("your notes say ×0.85") so the method is his and transferable.
  - Asking him to recall 1.15 rather than supplying it.
  - When Kai said "do i need the why", the tutor shrank the ask to "Is 15% of 68 more or less than 15% of 80?" instead of arguing. That is exactly right for this profile.
- Hurt or missed:
  - The "why" was scaffolded to a one-word answer, so Kai never articulated the general principle. A one-line generalising check would cost ten seconds and give the test Q1 explanation he lacked: "So if it went UP 20% first then DOWN 20%, higher or lower than the start?"
  - Like X, Z ended with "do Q4 and send it". After Kai's "k" it should have pre-empted the switch with the reverse-percentage trap: "Heads up: if any question says 'after a 10% increase it costs £66, find the original', don't take 10% off. Divide by 1.1. Want to check one?" That would have targeted misconception (b), which cost Kai a mark on the test.
  - The typo "Yes, no, it's £78.20" is minor but sloppy.

**Session Y**
- No teaching. The single most useful change would be to not lead with the final answer. Even within a fast format, ask Kai for the one calculator step (80 × 0.85) before showing the rest, and ask him to write the "why" line rather than putting it in a skippable paragraph.
