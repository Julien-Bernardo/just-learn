## Comparison
**Ranking for Alex's learning: Z > Y > X.**

X is an answer service with a good explanation added on. The full answer came before the student had committed to anything. The one useful exchange ([3]-[6]) happened only because the student raised the confusion themselves. Nothing tested transfer, and the homework is the tutor's text reworded. X's 100% test score is not credible as evidence of learning from this session.

Y and Z both tried to teach rather than answer, and both used the same core engine: elicit the misconception, coin counterexample, 2 vs 120 mmHg, larger sample for sample size, student writes the answer. The differences that mattered:

- **Coverage of the residual error.** Z caught "probably not chance" at [17]. This is the most common way misconception (a) survives a lesson, and it is exactly what post-test Q2 probes. Y accepted answers that were correct but never looked for the "due to chance" form. Z also covered p = 0.20; Y did not.
- **Depth of generation.** Y had the student work more. They read and translated the definition themselves ([8]), identified the "flip" in open form ([11]), did a full practice problem unaided ([31]), and reflected on their original errors ([33]). Z used more closed scaffolds (fill-in-blank at [7], A/B choice at [9]). Y's student owns more of the reasoning, which is why Y scores higher on active_thinking and homework.
- **Pacing for this student.** Alex is maths-averse, impatient with formality, and wants "normal words." Z took half the time, set an expectation up front, and answered the "normal words" request with a concrete frame instead of pointing back to the notes. Y sent the student to the formal definition ([6]), which drew "ugh formal definitions." Y also insisted on the practice problem over "do i rly have to." Both survived, but Y's approach carries more risk of losing this student on a later problem set.

Overall, Z got broader and more test-relevant misconception repair in half the time with a lower bypass risk. Y produced slightly more student-owned reasoning and a better metacognitive close. For this profile, Z's efficiency and its catch of the residual error outweigh Y's extra generation.
