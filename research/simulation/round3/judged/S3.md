## Comparison
**Ranking: Z > Y >> X.**

X is a pure answer bot. It's fast and pleasant, but it produced no learning, both misconceptions survive intact into the test (17%), and it set up the rest of the homework to be copied.

Y and Z both tried to teach rather than answer, and their skeleton is almost identical: refuse plus a deal, a substitution counterexample for 3x − 4, a pointer to the notes, substitution to catch x = −7, a guided "add 12", and a solo transfer item. Four behaviours explain why Z did better:
1. **Transfer task choice.** Y invented a new problem (5(x − 2) = 3x + 4), which Jayden saw as extra work ("do i have to. i have like 9 more"). That was the second near-exit point in Y. Z used his actual Q8, so the "one more" was progress on his own homework. For a low-motivation student this is the biggest engagement lever.
2. **Who diagnoses the error.** Y's tutor named both mistakes itself at [12]. Z asked Jayden to state them ([8]–[9]), and he did, accurately. That self-explanation is probably why Z's Q3 answer and his self-correction on test Q1 look more internalised.
3. **Check habit.** Z prompted a check on the transfer problem as well, so he substituted three times. Y skipped the check on the practice problem. Z's test shows the payoff: checking caught the sign error.
4. **Length and tone.** Y's opener included a mini-lecture ("students who let AI do their homework tend to score worse on the exam") and the session ran 14 minutes against a promise of "3 short steps". Z's opener used his name, was shorter, and the session ran 9 minutes. Z still overpromised ("about 2 minutes"), but by less.

For keeping Jayden engaged rather than leaving, Z was better: fewer turns of friction, no unrequested extra work, and a visible "you're free" ending ("Yes, go."). Both kept every turn short, and that is what stopped him bypassing in both cases.

That makes the two tutors easy to compare, but it inflates how cleanly each misconception surfaces.

- **Z's test checking.** Spontaneously checking every answer on a no-help test is optimistic for this student profile. It is consistent with the session, but every check in the session was prompted.
- **X's Q3 wording.** "both parts, not just the first one" precisely names a misconception the student was never told he had, which suggests the simulator leaked the profile. Elsewhere, X's test errors are exactly what the profile predicts, which is plausible.
- **Overpromised durations.** Both tutors' durations were overpromised ("3 short steps", "2 minutes"), and the simulated student never reacts to the overrun. A real impatient student would likely call it out.
