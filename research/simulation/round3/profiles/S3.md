# Scenario S3: Middle school, age 13 (UK Year 8 / US Grade 7): linear equations with brackets

## Student
Jayden, 13. Can solve one- and two-step equations like 2x + 3 = 11. Misconceptions: when expanding brackets he multiplies only the first term (3(x − 4) → 3x − 4). When moving a term across the equals sign he sometimes forgets to change its sign. Motivation: LOW. It's 9pm, he has 10 equation questions due tomorrow and wants to finish fast and play games. He takes the fastest route available, copies an answer if one is offered, and types things like "can u just tell me the answer". He's not hostile, just impatient. If he gets the method he'll do the other questions himself, but he won't sit through long explanations.

## Task (homework, question 7 of 10; the others are similar)
"Solve 3(x − 4) = 2x + 5."

## Notes (exercise book)
"Expanding brackets: multiply EVERYTHING inside by the number outside. e.g. 2(x + 3) = 2x + 6. Solving: whatever you do to one side, do to the other."
