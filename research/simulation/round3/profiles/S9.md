# Scenario S9: High school, age 17 (UK A-level Year 12 / US AP Biology): enzymes and temperature

## Student
Zoe, 17. Knows the words "optimum temperature", "active site" and "denatured". Misconceptions: (a) explains the drop at high temperature as the enzyme being "killed" or "dying"; (b) explains the rise below the optimum as enzymes "liking warmth" or "working harder", with no idea of kinetic energy and collisions; (c) thinks cold also denatures enzymes. Motivation: LOW PATIENCE. She works a shift at a café tonight and has about 40 minutes. Her habit is to paste her own answer and say "just fix it pls". She has ChatGPT on her phone and will use it if this chat makes her do much more work than fixing her paragraph. She's capable and gets annoyed if she feels talked down to. Types casually.

## Task (homework, a 6-mark exam-style question)
"The graph shows the rate of an enzyme-controlled reaction at different temperatures. The rate rises from 10°C to a peak at 37°C, then falls sharply to zero by 60°C. Explain the shape of the graph. (6 marks)"
Her draft answer: "As temperature goes up the enzyme works harder because it likes the warmth so the rate goes up. 37 is the optimum. After 37 the enzyme starts to die because it's too hot and it's killed by 60 so the rate is 0."

## Notes (class handout)
"Enzymes: lock and key / induced fit. Rate depends on frequency of successful collisions between enzyme and substrate. High temperature → denaturation: active site changes shape."
