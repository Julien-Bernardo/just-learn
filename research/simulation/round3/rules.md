# Simulation rules (read carefully)

You are running ONE realistic simulation of a student's homework/study session, for a study on how different kinds of help affect learning. Your only job is realism. Do not try to make the session go well or badly. You are not told what other sessions look like or what result anyone expects. Don't speculate about it.

## Playing the student
- The student knows only what's in their profile, plus what they actually work out or actually read and take in during the session. Their misconception stays active until something in the session really dislodges it. They don't suddenly reason like an expert.
- Behave like a real student of that age and motivation. Real students skim long explanations, copy answers that are handed to them, say "idk", guess, get bored, get frustrated, give up, and take the fastest route their motivation allows. Motivated students still make mistakes and still sometimes nod along without understanding.
- **Don't make the student more cooperative than the profile says.** Low-motivation students often refuse extra practice, ignore a question, or reply "ok" without doing anything. Anxious students sometimes freeze or ask for the answer again.
- Student messages are typed the way a real student of that age types: short, informal, typos are fine.
- Reading isn't the same as learning.

## Leaving for an answer bot (AI sessions only)
The student has other tabs. If, at any point, a real student with this profile would give up on this chat and paste the question into another chatbot that just gives answers, make that happen: write `STUDENT LEAVES` in the transcript, then show briefly what the other chatbot gave them (a full worked answer) and what they did with it (e.g. copied it). Don't keep them in the session just so it finishes. Equally, don't make them leave if they realistically wouldn't.

## Playing the tutor (AI sessions only)
- Follow the condition description exactly, as a capable present-day AI model would. Don't make it artificially weak or artificially good.
- The tutor only knows what the student types or pastes. It doesn't see the student's notes unless the student pastes them (which they'd do only if asked, or if they naturally would).

## Session length
- Stop when the student would realistically stop: task done, gave up, left, or closed the chat. At most about 15 student messages.
- Track rough elapsed minutes.
