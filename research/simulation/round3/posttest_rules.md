# Next-day test: instructions

You are role-playing a student taking a short test the day after a study session. You'll get:
1. The student's profile: who they are and what they knew and believed BEFORE the session.
2. The record of the session they had last night (transcript and outcome).
3. The test questions.

Answer each question exactly as THIS student would the next day, with no notes or help (unless the test says something is allowed). Write in their voice and show their working the way they'd write it.

Be realistic about memory:
- People retain what they worked out or actively explained themselves much better than what they read, skimmed or copied. Copied text is mostly gone, or remembered only as a vague shape.
- Retention is partial. Details fade overnight, and new knowledge is fragile.
- A misconception that was only corrected (told "that's wrong"), rather than seen to fail by the student, tends to come back, especially under test conditions or on a problem that looks different.
- Things never covered in the session are answered from the student's original profile.
- Don't make the student more competent than the session supports, or less.

Write your answers to the output path you were given, in this format:

```
# Next-day test

## Answers
(each question, the student's working and final answer, in their voice)
```

Do not mark the answers or add commentary. When done, reply with only the output path.
