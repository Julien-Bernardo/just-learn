TUTOR: Good start on finding the final speed, but the answer of 108 m isn't correct. Here's why and how to solve it properly.

**Where the approach goes wrong**

You correctly found the final velocity:

v = u + at = 0 + (3)(6) = 18 m/s

The problem is that `distance = speed × time` only works when the speed is **constant**. The car isn't travelling at 18 m/s the whole time. It starts at 0 and gradually speeds up to 18 m/s, so using 18 m/s for all 6 seconds overestimates the distance.

**Correct solution**

Since the acceleration is constant, use the equation:

s = ut + ½at²

Given:
- u = 0 m/s (starts from rest)
- a = 3 m/s²
- t = 6 s

s = (0)(6) + ½(3)(6²)
s = 0 + ½(3)(36)
s = 54 m

**Answer: 54 m**

**Alternative method (average speed)**

Because the acceleration is uniform, the average speed is the mean of the start and end speeds:

average speed = (0 + 18) / 2 = 9 m/s
distance = 9 × 6 = 54 m

Both methods give the same answer. Would you like me to explain how to choose between the equations of motion, or give you some practice questions?

**The key idea: 18 m/s is only the speed at the very end**

The car doesn't have a single speed during the 6 seconds. Its speed changes:

| Time (s) | Speed (m/s) |
|---|---|
| 0 | 0 |
| 1 | 3 |
| 2 | 6 |
| 3 | 9 |
| 4 | 12 |
| 5 | 15 |

18 m/s is only reached at t = 6 s. For most of the journey the car is going slower than that. That's why you got a number (108 m) that's too big.

**What the right speed to use is**

If you want to use `distance = speed × time`, you need a speed that represents the *whole* journey, the **average speed**. For constant acceleration, speed rises steadily, so the average is halfway between the start and end:

average speed = (u + v) / 2 = (0 + 18) / 2 = 9 m/s

distance = 9 × 6 = 54 m

This is exactly where the equation s = ½(u + v)t on your sheet comes from. The equation s = ut + ½at² is just that same idea rearranged using v = u + at.

**A quick check you can use**

If a car starts from rest, the distance should always come out as **half** of what you'd get using the final speed (here 108 / 2 = 54). That's a handy way to spot when your answer is too big.
