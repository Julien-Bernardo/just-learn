## Post-test (next day: no notes, no help, no AI)

(Equation sheet is allowed.)

**Q1. Cyclist at 4 m/s, accelerates at 2 m/s² for 5 s. How far?**
u = 4, a = 2, t = 5. First find v = u + at = 4 + 2 × 5 = 14 m/s. So distance = 14 × 5 = 70... no wait, that's the mistake from yesterday, she's not going 14 the whole time. Use the average speed: (4 + 14) ÷ 2 = 9 m/s. 9 × 5 = 45 m.
Check with the sheet: s = ut + ½at² = 4 × 5 + ½ × 2 × 25 = 20 + 25 = 45 m. Same. Answer: 45 m.

**Q2. Train slows uniformly from 20 m/s to rest in 10 s. How far?**
u = 20, v = 0, t = 10. Average speed = (20 + 0) ÷ 2 = 10 m/s. Distance = 10 × 10 = 100 m. The sheet has s = ½(u + v)t which is the same thing: ½ × 20 × 10 = 100 m. I don't need the acceleration here. Answer: 100 m.

**Q3. Explain why you can't use distance = speed × time with the final speed for an accelerating object.**
Because distance = speed × time only works when the speed is constant. When something accelerates its speed keeps changing, so it's slower at the start and only reaches the final speed at the end. If you use the final speed you get a distance that is too big. You have to use the average speed instead, like (u + v) ÷ 2, or use one of the other equations.
