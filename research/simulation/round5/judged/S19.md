## Session X

That is a strong move: the counterexample comes from Ben's own homework, and Ben is the one who breaks his rule. The tutor then walked him through a short chain of questions: what the diagram shows (more spread out) → does the same 1 kg take more or less space (more) → does mass ÷ volume go up or down ("down?? i think"). The tutor confirmed this with a numerical check (100÷50 vs 100÷100) and supplied the rule "less dense than water floats" itself [7]. Ben's first sentence was correct but thin [8]. The tutor asked him to add what stays the same and what changes, and his second version had the full same-mass/more-volume chain [9]. The tutor then had him say what his first idea was and why it failed [10], which is good metacognitive consolidation. For Q3, his first lazy attempt was "wood floats, rock sinks". One prompt ("use the same property") got him to a density-based answer [11–13].

**Scoring rationale.** Ben did roughly 40% of the reasoning. He made the key observations himself, but the tutor framed every step, and the float rule ("less dense floats") came from the tutor. The Q3 practice was only partly independent, because the log/pebble pair had already been used as the counterexample. He was largely rehearsing a conclusion he had already reached. Both submitted answers (Q2, Q3) are correct and in his own words. Q1, Q4 and Q5 were left undone, and the tutor only said "Q4 is just the formula", so the homework mark is middling. There was some grumbling ("do i have to"), but the turns were short and he stayed.

## Comparison
**Ranking: Y > X >> Z.**

X and Y used nearly identical scaffolds: the same opening deal, the same log/pebble refutation drawn from the page, the same diagram→volume→density chain, and the same "what was your first idea?" consolidation. Both reached 100% on the test as written. Two things put Y ahead:
1. **Y caught a real relapse.** In Y, Ben wrote "lighter" again after refuting it, and the tutor fixed it in his written answer. That is direct evidence of repair, not just assent. X's Ben happened to write "less dense" first time, so X's repair is less tested. It may be just as real, but the session doesn't show it.
2. **Y's independent practice transferred better.** Q4 plus the "float or sink vs 1 g/cm³?" extension is a fresh task that maps exactly onto test Q3, and Ben did it without help. X's independent Q3 reused the counterexample already worked through, so it was closer to repetition, although it does map well onto test Q1 (big heavy thing floats, small thing sinks).

On keeping Ben engaged, both were good and roughly equal. Every turn was one short question with an obvious next step. Y handled the explicit "can u just tell me" well by acknowledging it and saying how much time was left. Y also let him pick the practice question, which gives a low-motivation student a bit of control (he picked the easiest, which is fine). X's "do i have to" was handled by simply continuing. That worked, but there was less negotiation.

Z gave correct, high-quality answers to everything and taught nothing that Ben actually processed. It is the worst for learning, even though it gave him the most enjoyable short-term experience.

## Tutor behaviour critique
**Handling of the shared photo (all sessions).** The photo was clear, with no unclear regions and no links, so nothing needed flagging.
- **X** was the only tutor to confirm its reading of the page ("Have I read that right?"). That is good practice with photos, and it costs one line.
- **X and Y** both used the page itself as teaching material: Q3's log/pebble as the counterexample and the molecule diagram for the volume step. That is an excellent use of the shared material.
- **Neither X nor Y covered all five questions.**
  - Both left Q1, a trivial definition. They could have closed with "Q1: one line, what does density mean in your words?"
  - Both left Q5. That's a missed opportunity, because Q5 (most of an iceberg is underwater, yet icebergs are heavy) is the best remaining check on the weight-vs-density misconception and would take two turns.
  - Telling a low-motivation student to "try them yourself tonight" (Y) or saying "Q4 is just the formula" (X) almost guarantees those questions get copied from somewhere else.
- **Z** solved everything visible on the page, but as an answer engine. It was accurate, and its iceberg answer gave a correct 0.92 g/cm³ / ~90% explanation, but it didn't ask for or use any of Ben's own work.

**Using the student's own working.**
- **X** used Ben's lazy Q3 draft ("wood floats, rock sinks") well: "That's what happens, but it doesn't say why… use the same property." It also pushed his thin Q2 sentence to include what stays the same and what changes.
- **Y** used Ben's "lighter" draft brilliantly, tying it to his own refutation ("the word you just disproved with the log") and adding the heavy-iceberg counterpoint.
- **Z** looked at Ben's only piece of working ("is q4 just 2") just to add the unit.

**Session X**

- Hurt: at [7] the tutor stated "things less dense than water float" itself. It should have asked: "So ice has lower density than water.
