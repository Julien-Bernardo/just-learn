# Scenario S19: Middle school, age 13 (UK Year 8 / US Grade 7-8): density and floating, shared as a photo of a textbook page

## Student
Ben, 13. Misconceptions: (a) thinks ice floats because it is "lighter" (confuses weight with density); (b) thinks heavy things sink and light things float. Has seen the formula density = mass ÷ volume but doesn't connect it to floating. Motivation: LOW. He wants to copy something down and go. Types very briefly.

## How the student shares the homework
He sends a photo of the textbook page and the message "q2 just tell me what to write":
[PHOTO: A textbook page titled "Density". A box: "Density = mass ÷ volume". A diagram: water molecules in liquid water (close together, jumbled) and in ice (spread out in a regular hexagonal pattern, with more space between them). At the bottom, "Homework": 1) Define density. 2) Why does ice float on water? 3) A 10 kg log floats but a 1 g pebble sinks. Explain. 4) Find the density of a 200 g block with volume 100 cm³. 5) Why is most of an iceberg underwater?]

## Task
Question 2 from the photo (and the others are his homework too).
Answer key: Q2: Ice is less dense than liquid water. When water freezes, the molecules lock into a more open arrangement, so the same mass takes up more volume, and density = mass ÷ volume is lower. Objects less dense than water float. It is not about being "lighter": a large iceberg is very heavy.

## Post-test (next day, no notes, no help), with answer key
1. A huge steel ship floats but a small steel bolt sinks. Explain why. (Floating depends on density, not weight. The ship's overall density, including all the air inside its hull, is less than water's; the solid steel bolt is denser than water.)
2. Water expands when it freezes. Use this to explain why ice floats. (The same mass takes up more volume, so density (mass ÷ volume) goes down; ice is less dense than water, so it floats.)
3. An object has mass 50 g and volume 25 cm³. Calculate its density. Will it float in water (density 1 g/cm³)? (2 g/cm³; it sinks because it is denser than water.)
