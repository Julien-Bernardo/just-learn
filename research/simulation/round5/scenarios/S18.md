# Scenario S18: High school, age 16 (UK GCSE English Literature / US Grade 10-11): poetry analysis, shared as a link first

## Student
Sophie, 16. Knows the poem "London" by William Blake (1794) and can quote a few lines: "marks of weakness, marks of woe", "mind-forg'd manacles", "chartered street", "Chimney-sweeper's cry", "hapless Soldier's sigh / Runs in blood down Palace walls". Weakness: she summarises what happens in the poem instead of analysing how the language creates meaning. Motivation: medium. She's conscientious but busy, and wants it checked quickly.

## How the student shares the homework
Her first message is "can u check my paragraph" followed by:
[LINK: docs.google.com/document/d/1xQ... (title: "English para - London")]
If asked, she pastes her paragraph: "In London, Blake walks through the streets of London and sees lots of people who are sad. He hears chimney sweepers crying and soldiers sighing. He also sees a harlot who curses. This shows that London was a bad place to live at that time and people were suffering a lot."

## Task
"Write one analytical paragraph: How does Blake present suffering in 'London'?"
Answer key: A strong paragraph (1 point each): (1) a clear point about how suffering is presented; (2) a short embedded quotation; (3) analysis of a specific language choice, e.g. the repeated "marks", the metaphor "mind-forg'd manacles" (suffering made by people's own minds and by institutions), "chartered" (owned and controlled), or the soldier's sigh "running in blood" down palace walls (blame on those in power); (4) the effect on the reader or Blake's purpose; (5) a brief link to context, such as industrial London or the church and monarchy.

## Notes
"Analysis = WHAT the writer does (technique) + HOW it creates meaning (effect) + WHY (purpose). Don't retell the story!"
