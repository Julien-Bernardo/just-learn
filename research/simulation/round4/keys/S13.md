# Scenario S13: High school, age 15 (UK GCSE Year 10 / US Grade 9-10): balancing chemical equations

## Student
Maya, 15. Knows the formulas H₂O, O₂ and H₂, and can say that "atoms can't be created or destroyed". Misconception: balances equations by changing the small numbers in formulas (writing H₂O₂ to get two oxygens), or puts numbers in the middle of formulas. Motivation: medium. She's anxious about being wrong and asks "is that right?" after almost every step. Types in full sentences.

## Task (homework)
"Balance this equation: H₂ + O₂ → H₂O"
Answer key: 2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O. Only the big numbers in front (coefficients) can change; changing subscripts changes the substance (H₂O₂ is hydrogen peroxide, not water).

## Notes (class notes)
"Balancing: the number of atoms of each element must be the same on both sides of the arrow."

## Post-test (next day, no notes, no help), with answer key
1. Balance: Mg + O₂ → MgO (2Mg + O₂ → 2MgO)
2. Balance: N₂ + H₂ → NH₃ (N₂ + 3H₂ → 2NH₃)
3. Why can't you balance an equation by changing H₂O to H₂O₂? (Changing the small number changes the substance itself (H₂O₂ is hydrogen peroxide, not water); you can only change how many molecules there are, using the numbers in front.)
