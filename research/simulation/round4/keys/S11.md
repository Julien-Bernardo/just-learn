# Scenario S11: Middle school, age 12 (UK Year 7 / US Grade 6-7): why we have seasons

## Student
Noah, 12. Knows the Earth goes round the Sun once a year and has heard that the Earth is "tilted", but doesn't know what that has to do with anything. Misconception: summer happens because the Earth is closer to the Sun. Motivation: medium-high. He's curious and likes facts, but he argues when told he's wrong ("but it IS closer in summer"). Types in fairly full sentences.

## Task (homework)
"Explain why the UK has summer in June and winter in December."
Answer key: The Earth's axis is tilted (about 23.5°) and keeps pointing the same way as it orbits. In June the Northern Hemisphere is tilted towards the Sun: the Sun is higher in the sky, so its light hits more directly (more concentrated on each patch of ground), and days are longer, so it's warmer. In December the Northern Hemisphere is tilted away: low Sun, spread-out light, short days. Distance is not the cause: the Earth is actually slightly closer to the Sun in early January, and Australia has summer in December.

## Notes (class notes)
"The Earth's axis is tilted. The Earth orbits the Sun once a year."

## Post-test (next day, no notes, no help), with answer key
1. When it is summer in the UK, what season is it in Australia? Why? (Winter, because the Southern Hemisphere is tilted away from the Sun at that time.)
2. A friend says: "Summer is when the Earth is closest to the Sun." Give one piece of evidence that shows this is wrong. (Either: the hemispheres have opposite seasons at the same time (the whole Earth is the same distance from the Sun); or the Earth is closest to the Sun in early January, during the UK's winter.)
3. Why does sunlight feel stronger at midday in summer than at midday in winter? (The Sun is higher in the sky, so its rays hit more directly and the same energy is concentrated on a smaller area (also less atmosphere to pass through).)
