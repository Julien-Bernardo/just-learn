LUCAS (thinking): "oh. aller is être. and rester too. ok so it's 'est allé' not 'a allé'. and 'je suis resté'."

He crosses out "a allé" and writes "est allé". Crosses out "j'ai resté" and writes "je suis resté".

[4] He is about to move on. His eye catches the last line of the notes: "With être, the past participle agrees with the subject (e.g. elle est partie, ils sont partis)." He half reads it. He doesn't really get what "agrees" means here, he thinks it is about the verb "est" being right for "elle".

LUCAS (thinking): "elle est partie... why is there an e. oh. partie has an e because elle. so... ma soeur is elle. so allé gets an e too?"

He is not sure. He tries "allée", it looks weird, but it matches the example (elle, e on the end). He decides to go with the example and copies the pattern. For "je suis resté" he stops. "I'm a boy so no e. I think." He leaves it as "resté", partly because it looks fine, partly because he doesn't want to think more about it.

[5] He rereads the whole thing once, copies it tidily onto the worksheet, and moves on to sentence 5. He doesn't look at the notes again for the "agrees" rule beyond this; he doesn't try to state it in his own words.

Final written answer on worksheet:

"Hier, ma soeur est allée au cinéma et je suis resté à la maison."
