# Session S11

## Transcript

(Noah works alone at home at the kitchen table with his exercise book open to the class notes. No internet, no AI, nobody to ask. Think-aloud follows.)

[0] Noah reads the homework title: "Explain why the UK has summer in June and winter in December." He thinks: easy, this is the one about the Earth being closer to the Sun. He flips to his class notes and reads them: "The Earth's axis is tilted. The Earth orbits the Sun once a year."

[1] Noah (muttering): "Okay so it goes round the Sun once a year. And it's tilted. Hm. We did the tilt thing but I don't remember what it was for." He looks at the notes again. They don't say what the tilt does. He thinks about the diagram Mr/Ms teacher drew, a tilted ball with a line through it, but can't remember anything else about it.

[2] Noah: "Right, but summer is when it's closer, everyone knows that. It's hot because you're nearer, like standing near a fire. So in June the Earth is closer and in December it's further away so it's cold." He starts writing: "The UK has summer in June because the Earth is closer to the Sun in June."

[3] He stops and looks at the word "tilted" in the notes. "The teacher said it's tilted though, so I should probably put that in or it'll look like I didn't listen." He thinks about how to join the two ideas. He decides the tilt must be how the Earth gets closer: "Maybe the tilt means that part of the Earth leans towards the Sun, so it's closer to the Sun. So when the UK is leaning towards the Sun it's closer, that's summer." He is pleased with this because it uses both the notes and what he already believes.

[5] He writes: "The Earth is tilted and goes round the Sun once a year. In June the UK is tilted towards the Sun so it is closer to the Sun and it gets warmer, that is summer. In December the UK is tilted away from the Sun so it is further away and it is colder, that is winter."

[6] He rereads it. A small doubt: "But if it's tilted towards the Sun in June, then wouldn't the whole top half be closer...? And what about the bottom half, is that further? Does that mean they have winter then?" He pauses for about ten seconds. He doesn't know whether Australia is different (he vaguely thinks he might have heard Australia has Christmas in summer, but he isn't sure and decides it's probably a joke or just something his cousin said). "Anyway, it's the UK question, not Australia." He moves on.

[7] He thinks about adding more. "Should I say about days being longer?" He does half remember that days are longer in summer, but he treats it as a result of summer rather than a cause: "that's just because it's summer." He doesn't add it. He considers writing something about the orbit being slightly egg-shaped, because he's sure he read that in a book about space and it fits his idea, but he can't remember which way round it goes and doesn't want to get it wrong, so he leaves it out.

[8] He checks the notes one more time: the answer mentions tilt and orbit, which is what the notes have. "Good enough, it's got everything from the notes." Feeling reasonably confident, he closes the book.
