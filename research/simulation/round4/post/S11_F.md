# Next-day test

## Answers

**1. When it is summer in the UK, what season is it in Australia? Why?**

It's winter in Australia. Its the opposite because the Earth is tilted. When the top half (where the UK is) is tilted towards the Sun it gets summer, but then the bottom half is tilted away from the Sun so Australia gets the low Sun and the light is more spread out, and shorter days. So its winter there. Its the same planet so it can't be about distance.

**2. A friend says: "Summer is when the Earth is closest to the Sun." Give one piece of evidence that shows this is wrong.**

The Earth is actually closest to the Sun in January, which is winter in the UK. Also Australia has summer in December when we have winter, but its the same planet so its the same distance away.

**3. Why does sunlight feel stronger at midday in summer than at midday in winter?**

In summer the UK is tilted towards the Sun so the Sun is higher in the sky at midday. That means the light is more concentrated on each bit of ground, like when I shone the torch straight at the wall and it made a small bright circle. In winter the Sun is lower so the light is spread out over a bigger area like the stretched out dimmer torch patch, so each bit of ground gets less heat. (The days are longer in summer too so it has more time to heat up.)
