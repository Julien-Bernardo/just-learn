# Next-day test

## Answers

**1. When it is summer in the UK, what season is it in Australia? Why?**

It's winter in Australia. It's because of the tilt. When the UK is tilted towards the sun it's summer for us, but then Australia is tilted away from the sun so they get winter. Then it flips six months later and they get summer when we have winter. It's the same planet so it can't be about distance.

**2. A friend says: "Summer is when the Earth is closest to the Sun." Give one piece of evidence that shows this is wrong.**

Australia has summer in December when the UK has winter. The whole Earth is the same distance from the sun at the same time, so if being closer made summer then everywhere would have summer at once. Also I think the Earth is actually closer to the sun in January, which is in winter for us. (That's what my brother said too, but it doesn't work.)

**3. Why does sunlight feel stronger at midday in summer than at midday in winter?**

In summer the UK is tilted towards the sun so the sun is higher in the sky and the light comes in more directly. It's like the torch thing. If you shine a torch straight on a wall you get a small bright circle, so the light is more concentrated. In winter the sun is lower so the light comes in at a slant and gets spread out over a bigger area, so each bit gets less heat. The days are also longer in summer so there's more time for it to heat up.
