## Answers

**1. Balance: Mg + O₂ → MgO**

Mg + O₂ → MgO

Left: Mg 1, O 2. Right: Mg 1, O 1. So the oxygen isn't balanced.

I can't change the little numbers so I can't make it MgO₂. I have to put a big number at the front. I'll put a 2 in front of MgO, like the water one:

Mg + O₂ → 2MgO

Now O is 2 and 2. But Mg is 1 on the left and 2 on the right (2 × 1). So I put a 2 in front of Mg:

2Mg + O₂ → 2MgO

Check: Mg 2 and 2, O 2 and 2.

**Final answer: 2Mg + O₂ → 2MgO**

**2. Balance: N₂ + H₂ → NH₃**

N₂ + H₂ → NH₃

Left: N 2, H 2. Right: N 1, H 3.

Nitrogen first. I'll put a 2 in front of NH₃:

N₂ + H₂ → 2NH₃

Now N is 2 and 2. But H on the right is 2 × 3 = 6 and on the left it's only 2. Then I put a 6 in front of H₂?

N₂ + 6H₂ → 2NH₃

Check: H on the left is 6 × 2 = 12, and on the right it's 6. That's not the same. So it must be 3 in front of H₂, because 3 × 2 = 6.

N₂ + 3H₂ → 2NH₃

Check: N 2 and 2, H 6 and 6. I think that's right?

**Final answer: N₂ + 3H₂ → 2NH₃**

**3. Why can't you balance an equation by changing H₂O to H₂O₂?**

Because the little numbers show what the substance is. H₂O is water, but H₂O₂ is hydrogen peroxide, which is a different chemical (like bleach). So if you change it you're not making water any more, you're writing a different reaction. You can only change the big numbers at the front (the coefficients), because they just say how many molecules there are.
