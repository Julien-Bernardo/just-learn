## Answers

**1. A company has already spent £2m developing a product. Finishing it will cost another £1m, and expected revenue from it is £0.8m. Should it finish the product? Explain.**

the £2m is a sunk cost so it shouldnt count, its already spent and you cant get it back. so you only look at the future stuff. finishing costs £1m more and you only get £0.8m back so thats a loss of £0.2m. so no, it shouldnt finish it.

(i guess it feels like a waste after spending £2m but thats the sunk cost fallacy i think)

**Final answer: No, don't finish it.**

**2. Define a sunk cost in your own words and give an everyday example.**

a sunk cost is money (or time) that youve already spent and cant get back, so it shouldnt affect decisions you make now. rational decisions should only compare future costs and benefits.

example: buying a non-refundable ticket for something, like a concert ticket, and then not feeling like going. the money is gone either way so you shouldnt go just to not "waste" it.

---

**3. "We've already waited 40 minutes for this bus, so we should keep waiting rather than walk." Is this good reasoning? Explain.**

not really, its sunk cost fallacy. the 40 minutes is already gone whether you keep waiting or walk, so it shouldnt be the reason to stay. you should just compare whats ahead: how much longer youd probably have to wait for the bus vs how long the walk would take. if walking is quicker from now then you should walk, even though it feels like youve wasted the 40 mins.
