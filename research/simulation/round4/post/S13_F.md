**2. Balance: N₂ + H₂ → NH₃**

Left side: 2 N and 2 H. Right side: 1 N and 3 H.

I know I can't change the small numbers (I can't make it N₂H₃ or NH₆ or anything like that, because that would be a different substance). I can only put numbers at the front.

The nitrogens first: if I put a 2 in front of NH₃ I get 2 N on the right, which matches the 2 N on the left. But then H on the right is 2 × 3 = 6. On the left I only have 2 H, so I need 6 H. H₂ is 2 atoms each, so I need 3 of them, which means a 3 in front of H₂.

Check: left is 2 N and 3 × 2 = 6 H. Right is 2 N and 2 × 3 = 6 H. They match.

**3. Why can't you balance an equation by changing H₂O to H₂O₂?**

I actually did this on my first try yesterday. It does make the atoms match, because you get 2 oxygens on both sides. But H₂O₂ isn't water. It's hydrogen peroxide, which is a completely different substance. Changing the small number changes what the substance is. The equation is about making water, so the right side has to stay H₂O. The only numbers you're allowed to change are the big numbers in front of the formula, because they only change how many molecules you have and not what the substance is. So to get 2 oxygens you'd write 2H₂O instead, and then fix the hydrogens by putting a 2 in front of H₂.
