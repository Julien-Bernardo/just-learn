## Comparison
**Ranking: Z > Y >> X.**

X shows the baseline danger: alone, Noah made the misconception stronger by attaching tilt to distance.

Y and Z both repaired it, but differently:
- **Elicit first vs explain first.** Z asked for Noah's idea first and tested it. Y put the correct explanation and a model answer in the first reply, before Noah had said what he thought. His objection came after he already had a full answer, so it was handled as a rebuttal, not explored.
- **Who reasons.** Z let Noah turn the evidence into conclusions himself: the scale comparison [6], torch concentration [14], high Sun = straight torch [16], no tilt = no seasons [30]. Y's best moments ([6], [9]) came from Noah, but the tutor answered each with a full explanation instead of building on it.
- **Ownership of the homework.** Y overwrote Noah's draft with its own text. Z checked Noah's draft and probed it [23], so he submitted his own words.
- **Checking.** Z checked transfer (the no-tilt counterfactual) and had him say why his idea failed. Y only asked "Would you like me to explain any part in more detail?"

**Engagement.** Y was faster and nicer in the moment, with a lower risk that he'd look for an answer bot. Z cost twice the time and was a bit riskier: the tutor's answer to "it still could be closer overall" was another question, and Noah found the early part slow. Z kept him engaged by handling his pushback respectfully, telling him facts openly when needed ([7]), and giving him the "wait really??" surprise and a hands-on experiment. For a curious, argumentative student, that kind of engagement worked. Y builds dependence (Noah would come back for finished paragraphs). Z builds independence ("try it alone first and send me your answer").
