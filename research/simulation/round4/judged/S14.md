## Tutor behaviour critique
**Session X**

What helped:
- The "Go vs Sell" table with £40 in both columns [3] is an excellent, precise way to show the cost cancels out.
- The "gaining £25, not losing £15" reframe targeted the specific error she voiced.

What hurt:
- The first reply [0] gave the complete answer before Grace had said anything. The tutor should have asked what she thought first. A one-line "should the £40 matter? gut answer?" costs 20 seconds and brings out the misconception.
- It offered to write it up for her, twice ([0], [3]), before she asked.
- At [4] she asked for a short answer to submit, and the tutor gave her paste-ready text.

Concrete changes:
- When she asked for the short answer at [4], the tutor should have said something like: "You've got 2 minutes: write me two sentences: why the £40 doesn't matter, and what you'd do. I'll check it." Then it could correct her wording, not replace it.
- When she said "kinda get it", the tutor should have asked one quick check question (for example the bus wait or the cinema) before she left.

**Session Y**

What helped:
- A time-aware plan up front [0].
- Asking for her gut answer [0], which exposed the misconception plainly.
- Testing her claim with a concrete, answerable question about refunds [2], then reflecting her own answer back [3]. She generated "gone either way".
- Asking for a one-line description of each option [6], then pointing out that neither mentions the £40.
- Naming the fallacy *after* she had reasoned it out [10].
- Asking her to explain her original error [9]-[10].
- A short independent transfer item, with a calm, brief answer to "do i have to?" [11].

What hurt or could improve:
- At [7] the tutor nearly gave the answer away ("She said she'd rather stay home. So which option should she pick?"). It could have let her finish the opportunity-cost comparison herself.
- The transfer item was another paid ticket. When she wrote "still feels a bit weird", a *time* or *effort* sunk cost would have targeted transfer better and matched the test's Q3 type. Something like "you've waited 30 mins in a queue..." would do.
- "Still feels a bit weird" got a sympathetic answer but no check that the feeling had shifted. One line could have done it: "Does going make the £40 come back? So what's the feeling actually protecting?"
- The tutor switched the question's "you" to "she", which is harmless but slightly odd.
