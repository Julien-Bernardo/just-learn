## Tutor behaviour critique
**Y (teaching tutor)**

Moves that helped:
- At [1], the refusal was short and tied to his goal of finishing fast.
- The [3] praise was specific ("you've got the structure and the vocab").
- It sent him to his notes rather than telling him.
- At [8], when he said "idk what that means", it switched to a concrete question ("what got added to parti?").
- The self-explanation question at [12]-[14].
- A transfer sentence that changed both the verb and the subject's number.

Moves that hurt or wasted effort:
- It was quite step-by-step. At [4]-[5] it asked two near-trivial confirmation questions ("which helper verb does aller use?", "is rester in that list too?"). It could have said: "Check both your verbs against the list and fix the sentence."
- The [12]-[14] review ("what was your first idea… what was the second mistake?") adds two turns for a low-motivation student. One combined question would do: "Give me the two-step check you'll use on the next 9."
- At [15], when he wrote "ont arrivé" straight after the lesson, the tutor should have named the habit directly: "Your default is avoir. Make 'is it on the list?' the first thing you do for every verb." This is the durable fix for misconception (a).
- The ending let him leave without doing sentence 5. Better: when he said "ive got to go" at [17], ask for sentence 5 right then, in under a minute ("just the verb part: which helper and what ending?"). Otherwise, give him a 2-line checklist to keep next to the worksheet, so the remaining 9 sentences are not translator jobs.
- It accepted "im a guy so no" at [11] without making him say why (the participle matches "je" = male). That is a missed chance to make the agreement rule explicit for a subject that isn't elle.

**Z (answer bot)**

Moves that helped: the explanations were accurate and clear, the resté(e) answer was correct, and it offered a quiz.

Moves that hurt:
- It gave the full answer at [0] with no attempt asked for.
- It translated sentence 5 on request at [3].
- At [4] it actively agreed to translate all the remaining sentences ("paste them whenever you're ready").

Concrete changes:
- At [0], ask for his attempt first, or at least give only the auxiliary rule and let him produce the sentence.
- When he asked "is it resté or restée", turn it back to him ("who's 'je'? what does your notes' agreement rule say?") instead of answering.
- When he said "just gonna paste the rest", decline to bulk-translate and offer a quick check of his own versions instead.
