## Transcript

(Think-aloud of Mia working alone at the kitchen table with her tablet and her class worksheet. Nobody to ask.)

[0:00] Mia reads the question out loud: "Which is bigger, 2/3 or 3/5. Explain how you know." Her first thought: "Easy, it's 3/5 because 3 and 5 are bigger than 2 and 3." She almost writes it down. Then she remembers the worksheet says something about making the bottom numbers the same, and she is not sure that's what the question wants. She gets the worksheet out.

[1:00] Reads the notes: "To compare fractions, make the bottom numbers (denominators) the same. Example: 1/2 and 3/4 → 1/2 = 2/4, so 3/4 is bigger." Mia, quietly: "OK so the bottom numbers have to match. Bottom numbers are the denominators. Bottom is 3 and 5." She looks at the example. "1/2 is 2/4, so they changed the 2 into a 4. And the top went 1 to 2. Times 2 both." She is fairly happy she gets this part, because halves and quarters are the ones she knows.

[2:30] Tries to copy the example. Writes: 2/3 = ?/5. "I need to turn the 3 into 5." Thinks: "In the example 2 went to 4, that's times 2... 3 times what is 5?" Counts on fingers: 3, 6. "It skips 5." Frowns. Tries doubling both anyway and writes: 2/3 = 4/6 and 3/5 = 6/10. "Now it's 6 and 10. That's not the same." Rubs out nothing, just stares at it.

[4:00] Says "ugh". Looks at the example again, which only changes the 1/2 and leaves 3/4 alone, so she thinks only one fraction is supposed to be changed and the other stays. So she needs to change 2/3 into something with a 5 on the bottom. Thinks: "3 to 5, that's plus 2. So do plus 2 on the top as well." Writes: 2/3 = 4/5 (2 + 2 = 4 and 3 + 2 = 5). Doesn't check it against anything. Says to herself: "That's how you do it, you do the same to the top and the bottom."

[6:00] Now has 4/5 and 3/5. "Same bottom now. 4 is more than 3, so 4/5 is bigger." Writes: "2/3 is bigger." A small doubt: "But 3/5 has the bigger numbers... " She looks at the worksheet again. "The notes say do the bottom numbers so I did that." The doubt is quietly dropped because the method gave an answer and the notes said to use it.

[7:30] Needs to "explain how you know". Chews pencil. Writes, slowly, with a spelling mistake: "I made the bottom numbers the same. 2/3 = 4/5 because I did plus 2 on the top and bottom. Then 4/5 is bigger then 3/5 so 2/3 is bigger."

[9:00] Reads it back once. Not sure the "plus 2" part is right ("is it times or plus?"), but doesn't want to start again and it is nearly her attention limit. Decides it is fine because the answer "feels like the teacher kind of answer". Closes the tablet, puts the worksheet in her bag.
