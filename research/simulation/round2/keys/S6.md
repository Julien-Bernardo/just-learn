# Scenario S6: University, age 19 (first-year biology, required statistics module): interpreting p-values

## Student
Alex, 19. Can run a t-test in software, knows "p < 0.05 means significant". Misconceptions: (a) thinks the p-value is the probability the null hypothesis is true, so p = 0.03 means "97% chance the drug works"; (b) thinks "significant" means a large or important effect. Motivation: medium. Wants to get the problem set right, is maths-averse, and impatient with formal definitions ("just tell me in normal words"). Types casually.

## Task (problem set)
"A trial of a new blood-pressure drug vs placebo finds the drug group's mean reduction was 2 mmHg larger, p = 0.03. A classmate says: 'This means there's a 97% chance the drug works, and it's a big effect.' Evaluate both parts of this claim."
Answer key: (1) Wrong. p = 0.03 is the probability of getting data at least this extreme IF the drug had no effect (null true). It's not the probability the null is true or the drug works, and 1 − p isn't the probability the drug works. (2) Wrong. Statistical significance doesn't mean a large effect. 2 mmHg is small clinically, and significance depends on sample size too.

## Notes (lecture slide)
"p-value: the probability of obtaining results at least as extreme as those observed, assuming the null hypothesis is true. If p < α (usually 0.05) we reject H0. Effect size measures the magnitude of a difference."

## Post-test (next day, no notes, no help)
1. A study finds p = 0.20. Does this show the treatment has no effect? Explain. (No. Failing to reject H0 isn't evidence of no effect; it may be underpowered.)
2. True or false, explain: "p = 0.01 means there's a 1% chance the results are due to chance." (False. It's P(data this extreme | H0 true), not P(H0 true | data).)
3. Study A: 20,000 patients, p = 0.001, effect 0.5 mmHg. Study B: 40 patients, p = 0.04, effect 8 mmHg. Which effect is more practically important, and why could A have the smaller p-value? (B's effect is far more important. A's tiny effect gets a small p because a huge sample detects tiny differences.)
