# Scenario S4: High school, age 16 (UK GCSE Year 11 / US Grade 10-11): physics, motion with constant acceleration

## Student
Priya, 16. Knows speed = distance / time and v = u + at, and has a sheet listing the equations of motion, but doesn't know when to use which. Misconception: for an accelerating object she uses distance = speed × time with the FINAL speed (so here she'd find v = 18 m/s and say 18 × 6 = 108 m). Motivation: HIGH. Conscientious, wants to understand, exams in 6 weeks. But she's anxious: when stuck for more than a few minutes she panics and sometimes asks for the answer just to feel safe, then feels bad about it. Types in full sentences.

## Task (homework)
"A car starts from rest and accelerates uniformly at 3 m/s² for 6 s. Calculate the distance travelled."
Answer key: s = ut + ½at² = 0 + ½ × 3 × 36 = 54 m (or average speed (0 + 18)/2 = 9 m/s × 6 s = 54 m).

## Notes (equation sheet)
v = u + at
s = ut + ½at²
v² = u² + 2as
s = ½(u + v)t
distance = speed × time (for constant speed)
(u = initial velocity, v = final velocity, a = acceleration, t = time, s = displacement)

## Post-test (next day, no notes, but the equation sheet IS allowed, as in her exam)
1. A cyclist moving at 4 m/s accelerates uniformly at 2 m/s² for 5 s. How far does she travel in this time? (s = 4×5 + ½×2×25 = 45 m)
2. A train slows uniformly from 20 m/s to rest in 10 s. How far does it travel while slowing? (s = ½(20 + 0)×10 = 100 m)
3. Explain why you can't use distance = speed × time with the final speed for an accelerating object. (Speed isn't constant; it was lower earlier, so you need the average speed.)
4. A car speeds up uniformly from 5 m/s to 15 m/s over a distance of 50 m. What is its acceleration? (v² = u² + 2as: 225 = 25 + 100a, so a = 2 m/s². Tests choosing the right equation when no time is given.)
