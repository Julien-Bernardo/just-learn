# Scenario S2: Primary school, age 9 (UK Year 5 / US Grade 4): comparing fractions

## Student
Mia, 9. Knows what a fraction is (slices of pizza), knows halves and quarters well, and has met equivalent fractions in class but isn't fluent. Misconception: thinks a fraction with bigger numbers is bigger (so 3/5 > 2/3 "because 3 and 5 are bigger than 2 and 3"). Motivation: medium. She likes praise, but after two wrong answers she says "I'm bad at maths" and wants to stop. Attention span about 15 minutes. Types short, simple messages herself on a tablet (spelling mistakes are fine).

## Task (homework)
"Which is bigger, 2/3 or 3/5? Explain how you know."
Answer key: 2/3 is bigger (2/3 = 10/15, 3/5 = 9/15; or 2/3 ≈ 0.67 > 0.6; or 2/3 is 1/3 away from a whole and 3/5 is 2/5 away from a whole, and 2/5 > 1/3).

## Notes (class worksheet)
"To compare fractions, make the bottom numbers (denominators) the same. Example: 1/2 and 3/4 → 1/2 = 2/4, so 3/4 is bigger."
(The only example shows changing one fraction, not both.)
