**Question: How far was the Great Depression responsible for Hitler's rise to power?**

(working, rough notes first) depression = main one i think. nazi vote 2.6 to 37. unemployment 6 million. but treaty also, and propaganda. say why it matters

The Great Depression was the most important reason for Hitler's rise to power, but it was not the only one. The Nazi vote went from 2.6% in 1928 to 37% in July 1932, and this was because unemployment reached about 6 million by 1932 and people were desperate and wanted someone to blame and a strong leader who would fix things. The Weimar government seemed unable to cope so people stopped trusting it and voted for extreme parties like the Nazis who promised jobs and work. However, the Treaty of Versailles also mattered because many Germans already blamed the Weimar government for signing it, so the republic was weak before the Depression even started. Propaganda from Goebbels also helped Hitler get his message out. But without the Depression the Nazis probably wouldn't have got enough votes, so it was the main cause of his rise, although Hindenburg and von Papen making him Chancellor in January 1933 was the final step, so it was only mostly responsible.
