**1. Cyclist, 4 m/s, accelerates at 2 m/s² for 5 s. How far?**

Okay, what do I know. u = 4, a = 2, t = 5, and I want s. Those are exactly the letters in s = ut + ½at², so I'll use that one, like I did with the car yesterday.

s = ut + ½at²
s = 4 × 5 + ½ × 2 × 5²
s = 20 + ½ × 2 × 25
s = 20 + 25
s = 45 m

(I didn't just do 4 × 5 = 20, because she's accelerating so that wouldn't be right.)

**Answer: 45 m**

**2. Train slows uniformly from 20 m/s to rest in 10 s. How far?**

u = 20, v = 0 (it comes to rest), t = 10. I want s. I don't have a, so I'd rather use the one without a in it: s = ½(u + v)t.

s = ½ × (20 + 0) × 10
s = ½ × 20 × 10
s = 10 × 10
s = 100 m

I almost wrote 20 × 10 = 200 first, but the train is slowing down so the speed isn't constant, so I shouldn't use speed × time. To double check I'll do it the other way. v = u + at, so 0 = 20 + a × 10, so a = -2. Then s = ut + ½at² = 20 × 10 + ½ × (-2) × 100 = 200 - 100 = 100 m. Same answer, good.

**Answer: 100 m**

**3. Explain why you can't use distance = speed × time with the final speed for an accelerating object.**

My sheet says distance = speed × time is only for constant speed. If an object is accelerating then its speed is changing the whole time, so it isn't constant. The final speed is only the speed right at the end. For most of the time the object was going slower than that. So if you do final speed × time you're pretending it was going that fast the whole time, which gives you too big an answer. Like with the car I got 108 m with 18 × 6, but the real answer was 54 m.

I think the reason the other equation works is that it uses the average of the starting speed and the final speed (that's the ½(u + v) part), so it takes into account that the car started slow and sped up. 54 was exactly half of 108 because the average of 0 and 18 is 9. I'm not completely sure that's the proper explanation though, I was going to ask my teacher about it.

**4. Car speeds up uniformly from 5 m/s to 15 m/s over 50 m. What is its acceleration?**

u = 5, v = 15, s = 50, and I want a. There's no time given, so I need the equation with u, v, a and s but no t. That's v² = u² + 2as.

v² = u² + 2as
15² = 5² + 2 × a × 50
225 = 25 + 100a
225 - 25 = 100a
200 = 100a
a = 200 ÷ 100 = 2

**Answer: a = 2 m/s²**

(Check: average speed is 10, so it takes 50 ÷ 10 = 5 s, and 10 m/s gained in 5 s is 2 m/s². Yes.)
