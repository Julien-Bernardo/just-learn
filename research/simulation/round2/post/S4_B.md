**2.** Knowns: u = 20, v = 0 (comes to rest), t = 10. Want s. I don't know a, and I don't need it, so the equation without a is s = ½(u + v)t.

s = ½ × (20 + 0) × 10
s = ½ × 20 × 10
s = 100 m

(Average speed is 10 m/s and 10 × 10 = 100, so that seems sensible.)

**Answer: 100 m**

**3.** distance = speed × time only works if the speed is constant the whole time. When something is accelerating its speed keeps changing, so the final speed is only how fast it is going at the very end. For the rest of the time it was going slower than that. So if you do final speed × time you are pretending it was going at top speed the whole time, and you get a distance that is too big. You should use the average speed instead (for uniform acceleration), like in s = ½(u + v)t.

**4.** Knowns: u = 5, v = 15, s = 50. Want a. Time isn't given and I don't need it, so the equation without t is v² = u² + 2as.

15² = 5² + 2 × a × 50
225 = 25 + 100a
225 − 25 = 100a
200 = 100a
a = 2 m/s²

**Answer: 2 m/s²**
