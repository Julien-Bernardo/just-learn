## Answers

**Question: How far was the Great Depression responsible for Hitler's rise to power?**

(rough plan on the side: mostly responsible. evidence = unemployment 6 million, nazi vote 2.6 to 37. explain = people desperate. however = propaganda + treaty + von Papen. link back.)

The Great Depression was mostly responsible for Hitler's rise to power, although it wasn't the only reason. The Depression hit Germany from 1929 and by 1932 unemployment was about 6 million. At the same time the Nazi vote went from 2.6% in 1928 to 37% in July 1932. This mattered because millions of people were desperate and lost trust in the Weimar government that couldn't fix the economy, so they voted for Hitler who promised jobs and a strong Germany. Before the Depression the Nazis were tiny so without it he probably wouldn't have got anywhere. However, the Depression alone didn't make him Chancellor. Goebbels' propaganda helped the Nazis get their message out, and Hindenburg and von Papen made Hitler Chancellor in January 1933 because they thought they could control him. So the Depression gave the Nazis their support but the political deals decided it in the end, which means it was the main cause but not the only one.
