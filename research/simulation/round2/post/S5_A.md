## Answers

**Question: How far was the Great Depression responsible for Hitler's rise to power?**

The Great Depression was a major cause of Hitler's rise to power. The Depression hit Germany from 1929 after the Wall Street Crash. By 1932 unemployment was about 6 million. The Nazi vote went up from 2.6% in 1928 to 37% in July 1932. The Nazis also used propaganda and Goebbels to get support. This was significant because it made people really desperate and angry with the government so they voted for Hitler instead. Hitler promised to give people jobs and overturn the Treaty of Versailles, which got him more votes. However, this was less important than Hindenburg and von Papen because they made Hitler Chancellor in January 1933, so he got power because of them and not just the votes. Overall the Great Depression was quite responsible for Hitler's rise to power because it caused unemployment, but the Treaty of Versailles and Hindenburg were also important.
