**1. A study finds p = 0.20. Does this show the treatment has no effect? Explain.**

p = 0.20 is bigger than 0.05 so its not significant. so we dont reject the null. but i dont think that proves it does nothing, it just means the study didnt find enough evidence that it works. also p = 0.20 would be like a 20% chance the null is true (the treatment does nothing) so theres still around an 80% chance it does something. so no, it doesnt show no effect, its just not significant.

**2. True or false, explain: "p = 0.01 means there's a 1% chance the results are due to chance."**

True I think. p is basically the chance the null is true, and the null is like "its just chance / the drug does nothing", so p = 0.01 means 1% chance its just chance. which means 99% chance the result is real. (im a bit unsure because i remember the slide said something about "assuming the null is true" but i dont really get how thats different so im going with true)

**3. Study A: 20,000 patients, p = 0.001, effect 0.5 mmHg. Study B: 40 patients, p = 0.04, effect 8 mmHg. Which effect is more practically important, and why could A have the smaller p-value?**

Study B's effect is more important. 8 mmHg is a lot bigger than 0.5 mmHg, 0.5 is basically nothing compared to blood pressure of like 120. significant doesnt mean big, effect size is a separate thing from the p-value.

Why A has the smaller p: it has way more patients (20,000 vs 40) so i think the result is more reliable / more certain, so you can get a really small p even when the effect is tiny. B only has 40 people so the p is bigger even though the effect is large. (not totally sure on the exact reason but its something to do with sample size)
