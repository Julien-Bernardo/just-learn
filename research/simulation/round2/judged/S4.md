## Comparison
**Ranking for Priya's learning: W > X > Z ≈ Y.**

- **W** is best. She discovered the key idea herself (less than 108, then the middle speed), cross-checked with the sheet, explained the error in her own words, and then relapsed and self-corrected on the practice problem. That gives the strongest evidence of real repair plus a portable "check whether speed is constant" habit. It did so in 17 minutes and handled the panic without giving in.
- **X** got her to a similar end state (explaining why 108 is too big, solving a non-zero-u problem alone), but the central idea came from the tutor and a worked example after she broke down. It took 8 minutes longer and felt worse.
- **Z vs Y** is close, and they fail in opposite ways. Y has excellent self-generated strategy (variable matching, reading the caveat, cross-checking) but leaves the misconception intact and the student anxious. Z has clear concept explanation and the selection method, but no productive struggle and a copied homework. For *this* student, with a named misconception and an exam in 6 weeks, Z's explanation plus selection method is slightly more exam-useful. Y's self-found method is more durable but conceptually hollow. I give both overall 5, and rank Z marginally ahead on learning only.

**W vs X: which tutor behaviours made the difference.** Both refused the direct answer and both started from "was it going 18 the whole time?". The differences:
1. **Concreteness before abstraction.** W built the speed table (0, 3, 6, 9...) and asked for a prediction ("more or less than 108?") *before* asking for a representative speed. That made "middle speed" obvious. X jumped from "fair stand-in speed" straight to the word "average" without the table, and Priya guessed "3?". Same panic, different scaffold. W's left her one small step from the answer; X's left a gap only a worked example could fill.
2. **Response to the panic request.** W answered "you're one step away" and gave a hint that still left the insight to her. X answered with a full worked example, which calmed her but let her "just copy the steps" (her words).
3. **Checking as the default.** W made her verify with a second equation *before* confirming, and on the practice problem said "Don't tell me yet whether it's right". That surfaced the relapse and turned it into self-correction. X confirmed answers directly ("Yes, 54 m is correct") and only brought in the cross-check at the end.
4. **Practice design.** Both used a non-zero-u practice problem, which is right. W's happened to provoke the error again, which was valuable. Neither did a no-time problem, so equation selection (Q4) went untaught in both.

## Simulation realism concerns
- **Ceiling effect.** All four simulated tests score 100%, including Y, where the student explicitly ended the session not understanding why 108 was wrong. The test simulator seems to assume a capable conscientious student lands everything, which hides real differences. Y's Q3 especially contradicts its own session record.
- **W's practice problem is identical to test Q1** (4 m/s, 2 m/s², 5 s), so W's Q1 is recall, not transfer. X's practice is structurally the same as Q1 too.
- **Q4 in W and Y.** Neither session practised v² = u² + 2as, yet both students apply it fluently and add an average-speed check. W's is defensible (one "no v" selection hint); Y's check uses an average-speed idea she said she was unsure of. X's Q4, with a home-made route and an admitted gap, is the most believable.
- **X student weakness.** Being unable to give the average of 0 and 18 ([10]) after spotting that the speed rises evenly is a little extreme for a conscientious Year 11 student. It is plausible only as panic, which the transcript does signal.
- **Z student cooperativeness.** She asked "why is mine wrong" and "how do I choose" without prompting. That fits her HIGH-motivation profile, but it is the best case for an answer bot. A more typical anxious student who got "Answer: 54 m" would likely copy it and leave.
- **W timing.** W's own estimates ("about 5 minutes", "about 2 more minutes") don't match the 17 minutes logged. Minor, but it suggests the transcript timestamps were compressed.
