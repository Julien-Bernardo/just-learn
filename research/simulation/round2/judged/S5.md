Marking scheme used for every test (1 point per criterion, half credit if partial): (1) opening judgement, (2) specific accurate evidence, (3) explains HOW the evidence supports the point, (4) weighs against another factor, (5) sustained judgement in the link. I gave criterion 3 the same standard in every test. A one-step mechanism such as "people were desperate and lost faith in the government, so they voted Nazi" earns the point. Differences between the tests mostly show up in criteria 4 and 5.

## Comparison

**Ranking for Tom's learning: X > Y > W > Z.**

- **X and Y** are close. Both tutors refused to write the paragraph, both drew a judgement out of him early, and both got him to generate the counter-argument himself. The deciding differences:
  - **X let the misconception show itself, then made Tom diagnose it.** At [11] Tom wrote exactly the narrative he always writes. The tutor's question ("Could these sentences come from a textbook summary?") made him see the gap, and at [26] he named his own error in his own words. That is genuine repair of the target misconception (description vs analysis) rather than going around it.
  - **Y's questioning pre-empted the misconception.** Tom never wrote a narrative draft, so he never had to confront his habit. The analysis was assembled turn by turn under tutor control. He produced more causal reasoning in Y (higher active_thinking), but the scaffolding did more of the structural work.
  - **X chose factors better.** In X, Tom named the rival factors himself ([3]). In Y the tutor introduced the Depression ([7]) with a fabricated "you mentioned in class".
  - **Efficiency.** X took 28 minutes; Y took 38. At 10pm with a low-motivation student, that difference matters for bypass risk and for whether he comes back.
  - **Where Y beat X.** Y's [33] critique (the Depression explains power, not war) was the best piece of substantive historical feedback in either session. X's tutor accepted a paragraph with the same soft spot without comment. Y also rehearsed content that happens to match the post-test, which inflates its test result somewhat.
- **W** had honest effort and a flash of self-awareness ([5]), but no feedback. The handout stems became fill-in-the-blank forms, and the test shows the same template, the same descriptive opening block and the same contradictory conclusion. Learning was limited but genuine.
- **Z** produced the best-looking homework and the least learning. It also undermined the teacher's oral-explanation check, which was the one safeguard against this. Its 100% test is not credible as a measure of what Tom learned.

The push-back moments (X [4], [27]; Y [18]) are realistic but resolve quickly.

- **W is the most behaviourally realistic session**, and its test is the most consistent with its session.
