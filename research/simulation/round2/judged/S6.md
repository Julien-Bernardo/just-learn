## Comparison
Ranking for this student's learning: **W ≈ Z > Y > X**, with W slightly ahead on overall.

- **W vs Z** (both teaching tutors). Both repaired both misconceptions, both produced correct homework in the student's own words, and both got honest 100% post-tests. The differences:
  - **Z has more student generation.** The wet-ground analogy asked Alex to *map* the conditional himself ([12]). W's A-vs-B step only asked him to *recognise* the right statement ([10]). Z also flushed out "small p = big difference" and fixed it. That is why Z gets the higher active_thinking score.
  - **W is more efficient and better targeted at the traps.** W's coin example uses Alex's own prior to make 1 − p feel absurd. It caught "probably not chance", which is exactly the post-test Q2 trap. It also rehearsed the p = 0.20 case (Q1). W reached the same endpoint in 17 minutes against 29, with less irritation.
  - **The tie-break.** For a maths-averse, impatient student with medium motivation, the 12 minutes and the two friction points in Z ("ugh" after being sent back to the slide; "do i have to lol" at the summary request) carry real bypass cost in a real setting. That gives W the edge on overall, 9 vs 8.
  - **The tutor behaviours that made the difference.** In W: (1) handing over a fill-in frame *as soon as* Alex asked for normal words, instead of redirecting him to the notes; (2) a quick wording audit of the student's own answer. In Z: an analogy that the student had to map back himself.
- **Y vs X.** Y produced correct homework and a slogan-level fix of misconception (a) that will probably partly survive, but Alex's thinking was almost entirely bypassed. X was honest effort that ended by *writing down and submitting* misconception (a), so he walks away with it reinforced. Y ranks above X for learning, but neither is good. Y's realistic retention is well below its 100% test.
